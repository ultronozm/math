Siegel transforms and counting rational approximations on flag varieties

Abstract

Talk by René Pfitscher (Paris 13).

Dirichlet: infinitely many rational approximations with \[\left| x - \frac{p}{q} \right| < \frac{1}{q^2}.\] Khintchine: let \(\psi : \mathbb{N} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}_+\) be non-increasing. Then for almost all \(x \in \mathbb{R}\), the inequality \[\left| x - \frac{p}{q} \right| < \psi(q)\] has infinitely (resp. finitely) many solutions according as the sum \(\sum_{q \geq 1} \psi(q) q\) is infinite (resp. finite).

Schmidt, 1960: Set \[N_\psi(x, T) := \# \left\{(p, q) \in \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{N} :(\ast) \text{ holds, } q < T \right\}.\] Then for almost all \(x \in \mathbb{R}\), we have \[N_\psi(x, T) \sim 2 \sum_{q = 1}^T \psi(q) q \text{ as } T \rightarrow \infty.\]

Now let’s say a bit about flag varieties. More generally, let \(G\) be a semisimple \(\mathbb{Q}\)-group, and \(P\) a parabolic \(\mathbb{Q}\)-subgroup. We set \(X := G / P\). We choose a maximal compact subgroup \(K\). We also need a height function \(H_\chi : X(\mathbb{Q}) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}_+\). We also need a \(K\)-invariant Riemannian distance function \(d(\cdot, \cdot)\). We denote by \(\sigma\) the corresponding \(K\)-invariance Riemannian probability measure, given by the normalized volume form.

For example, we can take \[G = {\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}}_2(\mathbb{R}) > P = \begin{pmatrix} \ast & \ast \\ 0 & \ast \\ \end{pmatrix}, \quad K = \mathop{\mathrm{SO}}(2),\] so that \(X = \mathbb{P}^1\), and \[H : \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}_+\] given as follows: given \(v \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})\), we can represent it by a primitive lattice point \(\vec{v} \in \mathbb{Z}^2_{\mathrm{prim}}\), and we then define the height to be the norm of that vector: \[H(v) := \lVert \vec{v} \rVert.\]

We can picture this in terms of shortest integer vectors along rational lines.

Set \[N_\tau(x, T) = \# \left\{ v \in X(\mathbb{Q}) : d(x, v < H_\chi(v)^{- \tau}), \, H_\chi(v) < T \right\},\] \[\mathcal{E}(x, T) = \left\{ \vec{v} \in \mathbb{R}^2 - \{0\} : \lVert \vec{v} \rVert < T, \, d(x,[\vec{v}]) < \lVert \vec{v} \rVert^{- \tau} \right\}.\] In the example of interest, \[N_\tau(x, T) = \#(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{prim}}^2 \cap \mathcal{E}(x, T)) = \sum_{v \in \mathbb{Z}^2_{\mathrm{prim}}} 1_{\mathcal{E}(x, T)}(v).\]

We form the dyadic region \[\mathcal{F}(x, T) := \mathcal{E}(x, T) - \mathcal{E}(x, T/2).\] We can apply a flow, such as \(a_y := \mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(y^{-1}, y)\), to this region. We choose \(y_T\) so that the flowed region, \(\mathcal{B}(T)\), is nice and round. Then \[\begin{align} \#(\mathbb{Z}^2_{\mathrm{prim}} \cap \mathcal{F}(x, T)) &= \# \left( k_X \mathbb{Z}^2_{\mathrm{prim}} \cap \mathcal{F}(T) \right) \\ &= \# \left( a_{y_T} k_X \mathbb{Z}^2_{\mathrm{prim}} \cap \mathcal{B}(T) \right) \\ &\approx \mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\mathcal{B}(T)). \end{align}\] Here \(\mathcal{B}(T)\) “looks like a square”, while \(k_X\) is typically not “too distorted”.

Lemma 1. There exists \(\varepsilon> 0\) such that for all large \(T\), and all \(g \in G\) such that \(\operatorname{height}(g) \ll T^\varepsilon\), where \[\operatorname{height}(g) := \left( \min_{v \in \mathbb{Z}^2 - 0} \lVert g v \rVert \right)^{-1},\] we have \[\# \left( g \mathbb{Z}^2_{\mathrm{prim}} \cap \mathcal{B}(T) \right) = \mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\mathcal{B}(T)) \left( 1 + \operatorname{O}(T^{- \varepsilon}) \right).\]

Proof sketch. This is a counting argument due to (or at least inspired by) Eskin–McMullen.

Let’s first notice that what we want to estimate is, as we have said before, the primitive Siegel transform \[F_T(g) := \#(g \mathbb{Z}^2_{\mathrm{prim}} \cap \mathcal{B}(T)) = \sum_{v \in \mathbb{Z}^2_{\mathrm{prim}}} 1_{\mathcal{B}(T)}(g v) = \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma / \Gamma_\infty} 1_{\mathcal{B}(T)}(g \gamma e_1).\] Here \(\Gamma_\infty = \Gamma \cap U\), where \(U := \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 1&\ast\\ 0 &1 \\ \end{smallmatrix} \right)\). Now we unfold: \[\begin{align} \langle F_T, \phi \rangle &= \int_{G / \Gamma} F_T(g) \phi(g) \, d \mu(g) = \int_{G / \Gamma} \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma / \Gamma_\infty} 1_{\mathcal{B}(T)} (g \gamma e_1) \phi(g) \, d \mu \\ &= \int_{G / U} \int_{U / \Gamma_\infty} 1_{\mathcal{B}(T)}(g u e_1) \phi(g u) \, d u \, d m \\ &= \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} 1_{\mathcal{B}(T)}(k a_y e_1) \int_{U / \Gamma_\infty} \phi(k a_y u) \, d u \, d m. \end{align}\] Now we use the equidistribution result of Sarnak (1981) to see that there exists \(\alpha > 0\) so that for all \(f \in C_c^\infty(G / \Gamma)\), we have \[\left| \int_{U / \Gamma_\infty} \phi(k a_y u) \, d u - \int_{G / \Gamma} \phi \, d \mu \right| \ll y^{- \alpha} S(\phi).\] This yields the required asymptotic. The well-roundedness allows us to go from an average counting result to an individual counting result. Main term: \(f = 1_{\mathcal{B}(T)}\), \[\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\mathcal{B}(T)) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} f \, d m = \int_{G / \Gamma} \hat{f} \, d \mu.\] Variance: \[\int_{G / \Gamma} \left| \hat{f} - \mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\mathcal{B}(T)) \right|^2 \, d \mu = \int_{G / \Gamma} \left| \hat{f} \right|^2 \, d \mu - \mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\mathcal{B}(T))^2.\] The integral on the right hand side may be written \[\langle \hat{f}, \hat{f} \rangle = \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} 1_{\mathcal{B}(T)}(k a_y e_1) \int_{U / \Gamma_\infty} E(k a_y u, f) \, d u \, d m.\] This last integral is a constant term of an Eisenstein series. It describes second moments of Siegel transforms. One obtains in this way a bound on the variance. One can also argue and get an asymptotic estimate. ◻

Okay, here’s our result:

Theorem 2 (Pfitscher 2024). Suppose that \(P\) is a maximal parabolic subgroup of any semisimple algebraic \(\mathbb{Q}\)-group \(G\). Let \(\tau \geq 0\) be arbitrary. Write \(d := \dim X\), let \(\beta_\chi\) denote the Diophantine exponent, and define \[\Psi(T) := \begin{cases} T^{(\beta_\chi - \tau) d} & \text{ if } \tau < \beta_\chi, \\ \log T& \text{ if } \tau = \beta_\chi. \end{cases}\] Then there exists \(c > 0\) and \(\varepsilon> 0\) such that for almost every \(x \in X\), the counting function is given by \[\mathcal{N}_\tau(x, T) = c \Psi(T) \left( 1 + \operatorname{O}_X(\Psi(T)^{- \varepsilon}) \right).\]

Remark 3. For \(\mathbb{R}^n\), you can prove that the second moment of the Siegel transform is given as follows: \[\int_{G / \Gamma} \lvert \hat{f} \rvert^2 \, d \mu - \left( \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f \, d m \right)^2 = \int_{\mathbb{R}} (\lvert f \rvert^2 + f \cdot f^-) \, d m \ll \mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\mathcal{B}(T)),\] where \[f^-(v) = f(- v).\] This estimate suffices to get the conclusion of the theorem (for an arbitrary decreasing \(\Psi\)). Kelmer–Yu gave an analogue for the sphere. Our proof used dynamics.
